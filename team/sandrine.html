Sandrine Laurance, PhD is a Research Scientist leading an research program within the BaHSic team. Her work explores how bone marrow microenvironments regulate erythropoiesis under stress conditions, with a particular focus on sickle cell disease and aging-related anemia.

Research interests

  • Erythropoiesis and red blood cell biology
  • Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells
  • Inflammatory anemia

Key expertise

  • Erythropoeisis
  • Flow cytometry
  • Proteomic and transcriptomic

Background

  • CRCN, Inserm UMR_S1134 (2021–Now)
  • Post-doctorat, UMR_S1134 (2018–2021)
  • Post-doctoral Fellow, Lady Davis Institute, Université McGill (Canada) (2010–2018)
  • PhD – Université Paris Cité (2008) : Pharmacogenomic approach of hydroxyrurea treatment in Sicckle Cell Disease

Current projects

Her current projects combine cellular biology, proteomics, and functional models to investigate interactions between erythroid cells and their niche in a physiological but also in pathological context (sickle cell disease, inflammaging)
